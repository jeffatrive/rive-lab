Accessibility

VECTOR/9 is a made-up 2003 design agency, built as a Rive showcase: an intro, a soundtrack, an orbiting menu and four pages, all drawn in one Rive file on a canvas. This page says what works for people using a keyboard, a screen reader or reduced motion, and what doesn't yet. The aim is WCAG 2.2 level AA.

Keyboard

  • Tab and Shift+Tab move between controls; a white ring shows where you are.
  • Enter or Space presses a button or flips a switch.
  • Escape skips the intro, closes a project, and goes back from a page to the menu.
  • On the LAB page, the arrow keys spin the 3D emblem once it has focus.
  • When a screen or page opens, focus moves to its heading. When a page or project closes, focus goes back to what opened it.

Screen readers

The page turns on Rive's accessibility layer, so the site is made of real headings, buttons, switches, text fields, a dialog and a status message rather than a blank canvas:

  • each screen and page has a heading, and the projects, skills and studios are in labeled groups;
  • the menu tags, project cards and LAB settings, which are drawn by scripts, have labeled controls in their place;
  • the project details open as a dialog that holds focus until it closes;
  • the contact form's fields are labeled, and its "sending" steps are announced as they happen.

Motion

The MOTION switch at the top right turns off the site's animation. It starts off if your device asks for reduced motion. With motion off:

  • the intro, which has two bright flashes, is skipped;
  • the menu holds still, and pages change with a quick fade instead of the scan and the fly-in;
  • pages appear all at once, and the project pictures only play while you point at or focus them;
  • the LAB emblem stops spinning, and the music readout stops scrolling.

Sound

Nothing plays until you choose "Enter with sound". The SOUND switch and the music player's STOP button are always at the top right. The sound is music and effects only; there's no speech.

Color and size

  • Text meets the 4.5:1 contrast ratio, except decorative readouts that aren't needed to use the site, and the outlines of the text fields meet 3:1.
  • The layout reflows to fit the window, so browser zoom on a computer makes everything larger.

Known gaps

  • On phones and tablets, pinch zoom doesn't work: drags on the site are used for drawing, spinning and scrolling.
  • The drawing toy on the loading screen needs a pointer or a finger. It's optional; loading carries on without it.
  • The 3D emblem and the project pictures are described in words; their details aren't.
  • The screen-reader experience has been checked with the browser's accessibility tools and the keyboard, but not yet with VoiceOver, NVDA or JAWS.
